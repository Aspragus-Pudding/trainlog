# Lift-Specific Diagnosis and Specialization for an Adaptive Strength App: Squat, Bench, Deadlift, Overhead Press, Weighted Pull-Up

**Bottom line: when a lift stalls, the app's default should be more practice of the stalled lift itself — more weekly sets and sessions, and a progression that fits — and it should switch to a targeted variant only when logged data shows a specific mismatch, such as variant-to-lift ratios that are drifting, failures in the same place every time, or pain.** The specificity evidence is strong (grade A). The evidence that a weak-point variant fixes a particular sticking point is weak (C/D). Sticking regions are largely a built-in mechanical feature of each lift, not a defect you can remove.\[1\]

## TL;DR
- **Specificity vs variation:** strength gains are strongly task-specific. A 2025 Sports Medicine meta-analysis (Saeterbakken et al.) found dynamic strength rose with a pooled SMD of 0.98 (95% CI 0.91–1.06), against 0.42 (95% CI 0.35–0.49) for transfer to untrained isometric tests (**grade A**). Close variants transfer partially, and training through a full range of motion beats partials over 10 weeks (**B**). No study has validated a rule for deciding which stalled lifter needs a variant instead of more of the main lift (**D**). The app should default to "more competition lift" and send users to a variant only on specific logged signals.
- **Diagnosis:** load, reps, RPE, variant ratios and pain ratings can show *where the capacity is missing* (for example paused bench lagging touch-and-go, or front squat lagging back squat) and *whether fatigue explains the stall*. They cannot show bar path, speed through the range, or where in the rep the lift failed. For those, use a short question ("Where did the rep slow or stop?") or a prompt to record a video.
- **Programs and cases:** the respected specialization methods are high-frequency practice of the main lift (Smolov, Bertrand's daily squatting) or main lift plus heavy close variants (Coan/Phillipi). They produce real but often temporary gains, at high cost to recovery and joints.\[2\]\[3\] The few documented long plateaus on squat, deadlift and press broke through either more specific practice or, in one deadlift case, *less* competition-lift practice combined with maxing on variants.\[4\] That contradicts any single rule.

## Key Findings

### 1. Specificity vs variation — the central answer
**What the evidence says**
- **Task specificity is the strongest finding in this area (A).** Saeterbakken et al. (Sports Medicine 2025, systematic review and meta-analysis) found dynamic training raised dynamic strength with a "two-fold larger effect size" than its transfer to untrained isometric strength (SMD 0.98, 95% CI 0.91–1.06, vs 0.42, 95% CI 0.35–0.49); the authors describe the two as "different neuromuscular domains".
- **Transfer between related lifts is real but incomplete (B).** In an NSCA Strength & Conditioning Journal review (2022), untrained men randomized to squat, leg press, or both all improved their squat 1RM, but the squat-only group improved most. A rear-foot-elevated split squat group still added 10 kg to back squat without back squatting.\[5\] Across studies, the trained exercise gains most and close relatives gain partly.
- **Variant choice changes general strength somewhat (B).** In recreationally trained women (Enes et al., 2024), back squat training improved 45° leg press strength more than front squat training, with similar quad growth.\[6\]
- **Moderate variation doesn't hurt (B).** In an 8-week RCT (Baz-Valle et al., PLOS ONE 2019), 21 resistance-trained men whose exercises were "randomly varied each session via a computerized app" made bench and squat gains similar to a fixed program, with better motivation. That trial didn't test plateaued lifters.
- **Range-of-motion partials (B, with disagreement).** A 10-week study (Martínez-Cava) found full-ROM bench beat partial-ROM for maximal strength.\[7\] Stronger By Science's review notes that the ⅓-ROM group had the *worst* gains even at ⅓ ROM in three of four measures.\[8\] A 2026 4-week study using supramaximal (105%) progressive board-style presses, with the bar stopping 5″ to 2″ above the chest, found 1RM gains similar to full-ROM training, but the bar kinematics changed only after full-ROM training.\[9\]\[10\]\[11\] Partials can match full ROM over short blocks in experienced lifters. They don't beat it, and they don't retrain the bottom.

**What respected coaches do (D)**
- Stronger By Science (Nuckols) says the squat sticking point "is probably never going to go away" and the fix is to "keep squatting and get stronger". The aim is stronger knee and hip extensors overall, not attacking the slow spot.\[1\]
- Powerlifting-to-Win's critique of Coan/Phillipi praises its "real deadlift volume" and is lukewarm on its heavy load of non-specific assistance work.\[12\]
- Conjugate and Westside-style coaches do the opposite and rotate max-effort variants (see the StrongerYou deadlift case).\[4\] These are two genuinely different schools of thought, and neither has trial evidence for plateaued lifters.

**The rule the app should use (synthesis, D, built on A/B foundations)**
1. **First rule out non-specific causes:** too little weekly practice of the lift (fewer than 2 sessions a week or very few hard sets), weight jumps too big for the lift, accumulated fatigue (readiness trending down, RPE rising at the same load), or bodyweight falling. The 2025 meta-analysis and the plateau cases show these are the most common fixable causes.
2. **Default fix: more of the stalled lift.** Add one weekly session or about 30–50% more hard sets.\[13\] Keep most sets at RPE 6–8 and use smaller jumps or double progression. Run it for 4–8 weeks.
3. **Switch to a targeted variant only if one of these holds:** (a) a variant ratio is out of the user's own range and getting worse, (b) the user consistently reports failing at the same place, (c) pain limits the main lift, or (d) four or more weeks of extra main-lift practice produced no change in estimated 1RM. Even then, the variant should *replace part of* the main-lift volume (about a third to half of it), not all of it. It should be the closest variant that addresses the issue: a pause, tempo, a small ROM change or a grip change, before anything like boards or bands.

### 2. Sticking points: what biomechanics actually shows
- **Bench (B):** a sticking region appears at loads above roughly 80% 1RM, starting shortly after the bar leaves the chest.\[14\] Van den Tillaar's lab found force output dips in this region even in isometric and pure-concentric presses. Their conclusion is that it's "most likely the result of a poor mechanical force position," and not a loss of stretch-reflex (elastic) potentiation.\[15\]\[16\]\[17\] Failures don't always happen inside the sticking period.\[16\] Grip width changes the joint moments,\[18\] and a 2025 link-chain model (Evangelista et al., Journal of Functional Morphology and Kinesiology) found that "grip width and barbell constraint had a greater effect on shaping the sticking region than muscle architecture parameters"; in its simulation, adding elbow torque "shifted the velocity minimum from 38 cm to 23 cm above the chest". That supports the view that technique and setup matter (C).
- **Squat (B):** in free-weight 6RM squats, only 10 of 15 participants showed a sticking region at all (van den Tillaar et al., Journal of Human Kinetics 2014), and it was "much shorter than in the bench press". Quad activity falls and glute activity rises through it, so it looks like a hand-off between the knee and hip extensors.\[19\]\[20\] Bar position (high vs low) didn't change the sticking-region kinematics. A 2025 study found relative strength doesn't change where the sticking region falls.\[21\] A 2026 study found the preceding descent boosts force in the sticking region, which supports a controlled but elastic descent over dead-stop squats for competition-style strength (C).\[22\]
- **Deadlift (B/C):** in competition, the conventional sticking point fell at a thigh angle of about 57°, near the knees, with minimum bar velocity of 0.20 m/s against 0.09 m/s in the squat (Hales et al., 2009).\[23\] Momentum fades *before* the knees.\[24\] The authors concluded squat and deadlift are "markedly different (p < 0.01), implying that no direct or specific cross-over effect exists", which argues against assuming squat work fixes the deadlift.
- **Overhead press (C):** the same 2025 model (Evangelista et al.), which simulated both bench and overhead press, found the press slows when "shoulder torque dropped below a critical threshold". There are almost no experimental sticking-region studies on trained pressers. Coaching convention puts typical failures at forehead-to-crown height.
- **Weighted pull-up (D):** no sticking-region research exists. Coaching convention puts most failures at the start (the dead hang) or the top (chin over the bar).

### Squat
| Sticking point | Likely cause | Signal / question | Recommendation | Programming |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bottom ("in the hole") | Low leg strength at depth; losing tightness; bouncing without control | [Log] paused squat under ~85–90% of the user's back squat or falling; [Ask] "Did it stop right out of the bottom?" | Mostly more back squat practice; add paused squat as the second squat slot | Pause squat 2-sec, 3–5×3–5 @ RPE 7–8, once a week, 6–8 weeks, replacing about a third of squat volume (C/D)\[13\] |
| A few inches above parallel (the typical sticking region) | Normal mechanics (knee-to-hip hand-off); quads or adductors limiting | [Log] front:back squat below the user's baseline; [Ask] "Did it slow 2–6 inches above parallel?" | More squat volume and frequency; front squat or high-bar as a quad-biased variant | Front squat 3–4×4–6 @ RPE 7–8, 1×/week; leg press or hack squat 3×8–12 (B for general transfer, D for targeting the point) |
| Hips shoot up / chest falls ("good-morning squat") | Quad weakness or technique | [Video] needed; [Ask] "Did your hips rise before the bar?"; [Log] front squat lagging | Technique cue plus quad-biased variant\[25\] | Tempo squat 3-1-0, 3×5 @ ~65–70%; front squat (D) |
| Fails only in late sets / RPE rises at the same load | Fatigue, not a weak point | [Log] readiness trend, RPE drift of 1+ at a matching load over 2–3 weeks | Deload or cut volume, then resume\[26\] | Cut sets ~40% for a week (D) |
| Pain-limited | Knee or hip irritation | [Log] joint pain ≥4/10 on squat days | Box squat, tempo, belt squat or leg press substitution | Keep weekly squat-pattern volume while staying under the pain threshold (D) |

### Bench press
| Sticking point | Likely cause | Signal / question | Recommendation | Programming |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Off the chest | Relying on the bounce; poor tightness at the bottom; pec and front-delt strength at length | [Log] paused/touch-and-go ratio below ~0.93 or falling; [Ask] "Did it stall right off your chest?" | More paused bench; Spoto press or long-pause bench\[27\] | Paused bench as the main movement 2–3×/week; 3-sec pause sets 3×3–5 @ RPE 7–8 (C) |
| Mid-range (normal sticking region, a few inches up) | Normal mechanical disadvantage; grip and elbow path | [Ask] "Did it slow mid-way?"; [Video] for bar path | More frequency (bench responds well to 2–4×/week); grip check | Add a session; occasional pin press at the sticking height, 3×3 @ RPE 8, 4–6 weeks (C/D) |
| Lockout | Triceps | [Log] close-grip/competition ratio below ~0.88–0.90; [Ask] "Did it stop in the last third?" | Close-grip bench; board or pin press | Close-grip 3–4×4–8 @ RPE 7–8; boards 105% top singles for at most 4 weeks (B for short-term transfer) |
| Shoulder pain | Too much horizontal abduction, flared elbows, bouncing | [Log] shoulder pain ratings | Floor press, Spoto, narrower grip, neutral-grip DB | See the constraints section |

### Deadlift
| Sticking point | Likely cause | Signal / question | Recommendation | Programming |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Off the floor | Leg drive at the start; set-up position | [Log] deficit/conventional ratio below ~0.90; [Ask] "Couldn't break the floor?" | Deficit deadlift (1–2″) or paused deadlift (below the knee)\[28\] | Deficit 3×3–5 @ RPE 7–8, 1×/week, 6–8 weeks (C) |
| Just below or at the knee (the typical sticking point) | Normal mechanics; back position lost; slow start | [Ask] "Did it slow at the knees?"; [Video] for hips shooting up | More deadlift volume; paused deadlift at the knee; RDL | Coan-style heavy doubles plus speed sets (D)\[29\] |
| Lockout | Glutes or upper back; grip | [Log] rack pull far above the conventional max; [Ask] "Did it fail above the knees, or did your grip fail?" | Hip thrust or RDL; grip work if grip is the limit | Rack pulls are popular but have little transfer evidence (D) |
| Meet or test-day misses, gym PRs fine | Fatigue and peaking | [Log] gym performance good, test misses | Fatigue management (StrongerYou case)\[4\] | Lower competition-pull frequency; taper (D) |

### Overhead press
| Sticking point | Likely cause | Signal / question | Recommendation | Programming |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Off the shoulders | Delt and upper-chest strength; rack position | [Ask] "Did it stall right off your shoulders?" | Pause press, pin press from the bottom, push press for overload\[30\] | Pause press 3×3–5 @ RPE 7–8 (D) |
| Forehead to top of head | Bar path around the head; triceps; delts at end range | [Video] for head/bar path; [Ask] "Did it stop at forehead height?" | Technique (head through), plus triceps work | Pin press at forehead, 3×3; overhead triceps work 3×10 (D) |
| Stuck at small loads | Weight jumps too big | [Log] 2.5 kg = 5%+ of the press | Microplates and double progression\[30\]\[31\] | 0.5–1.25 kg jumps; rep ranges like 3×3–6 (C/D) |
| Press drops with bodyweight | Bodyweight sensitivity | [Log] bodyweight trend | Expect it; maintain volume | Michael Wolf reports the press is the first lift lost when bodyweight drops (D)\[32\] |

### Weighted pull-up / chin-up
| Sticking point | Likely cause | Signal / question | Recommendation | Programming |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dead hang (start) | Scapular depression and lat strength at length | [Ask] "Couldn't start the pull?" | Scapular pull-ups; paused-start pull-ups | 3×5 scapular pulls; 2-sec dead hang before each rep (D) |
| Top (chin over bar) | Elbow flexors and upper back | [Ask] "Couldn't get your chin over?"; [Log] chin-up vs pull-up ratio | Chin-ups; top holds; rows | Top isometric holds 3×5–10 sec; heavy rows (D) |
| Load stalls, bodyweight rising | Total system load increased | [Log] bodyweight + added load trend | Track total load (bodyweight + plate), not just the plate | Report system-load progress (D) |
| Overtraining sign | Elbow pain, high frequency | [Log] elbow pain | Cut frequency (Rippetoe's advice)\[33\] | 2×/week max heavy (D) |

## Specialization Programs

| Program | Structure | Key numbers | Reported results | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Smolov (squat)** | 13 weeks; base cycle 4×/week\[34\] | 4×9 @70%, 5×7 @75%, 7×5 @80%, 10×3 @85%, adding about 10 kg/week\[2\] | Tanner Jones: 215→260 kg across meets; James Clear: failed 405 lb, succeeded 7 weeks later; reddit report 270→330 lb\[2\]\[35\]\[36\] | Powerlifting-to-Win: "highest burnout rate…and higher incidence of injury"; gains often temporary (Jones never matched it again); demands eating and sleep\[2\]\[3\] |
| **Smolov Jr. (bench/squat)** | 3–4 weeks; 4×/week | 6×6 @70%, 7×5 @75%, 8×4 @80%, 10×3 @85% | Claims of 30–60 lb squat and 15–25 lb bench (marketing, D)\[37\] | Joint stress, especially shoulders on bench |
| **Coan/Phillipi (deadlift)** | 10 weeks; 1 deadlift session/week\[29\] | Heavy single/double @75–100%; speed pulls 8×3 @60–75%; stiff-leg deadlifts and back work\[38\] | Philippi +35 lb; berserkbarbell 260→290 kg in 11 weeks; general claims 20–50 lb\[29\]\[39\] | Hard to finish for masters lifters; non-specific assistance volume\[12\] |
| **Bertrand daily squat (coach Willie Albert)** | 6 weeks of daily squatting\[40\] | 10 singles @80–87% alternating with max days\[40\] | Bench PR and deadlift PR; 500 lb squat at the meet (not a confirmed squat PR)\[40\] | Extreme pain reported\[40\] |
| **StrongerYou conjugate deadlift** | 5-month prep\[4\] | Trap bar max effort, then banded deficit sumo, then a 4-week peak | 500 lb meet "curse" broken (501 lb on second attempt, a 10 kg meet PR)\[4\] | Built for one lifter |
| **Press frequency/microloading** | 2×/week heavy and volume days\[30\] | Microplates, double progression | T-Nation reports: press moving again on 5/3/1; 70 kg→76 kg×2 in 9 months\[31\] after a year stuck\[31\] | Slow by nature\[31\] |
| **Streetlifting weighted pull-up** | 3–4×/week | RPE-based singles, triples and volume sets\[41\]\[42\] | Mostly marketing claims of +10–30 kg\[43\] | Elbow overuse |

**Principles that hold across programs**
- **High frequency of the actual lift** (Smolov, Bertrand, the press reports) is the common factor in breakthroughs (C).
- **Gains are bigger after a period of low volume** (Powerlifting-to-Win on Coan/Phillipi: "explosive gains" for people used to low deadlift volume).\[12\] Specialization works partly because it removes the detraining in that lift (D).
- **Other lifts are kept at maintenance** (Smolov: "great for the squat, is going to hurt your other two lifts").\[3\]
- **Many gains are temporary peaks** (Jones).\[2\] The app should re-test 4–8 weeks after a specialization block to see what was kept.

| **Overhead press – Michael Wolf (Starting Strength coach)** | Stuck at 265 lb for about 2 years, with shoulder surgery in between\[44\] | No specific variable | 265→290 in about a month, then 300, then 315 over about 2 years\[44\] | "months or even years of work to produce a few PRs"\[44\] |

The cases are few and mostly anecdotal (D). No documented weighted pull-up breakthrough with full details was found.

## Diagnostic Ratios

| Ratio | Typical range | What out-of-range suggests | Reliability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paused / touch-and-go bench** | ~0.93–0.95 (peer-reviewed: 62.3 vs 66.5 kg in 96 men, ≈0.94; coaches say 2–7% gap)\[45\]\[46\] | Below ~0.92: relying on the bounce, weak at the bottom | C – one sample of novices |
| **Close-grip / competition bench** | ~0.88–0.95 (coach convention) | Low: triceps; high: pecs or shoulders limiting the wide grip | D |
| **Front / back squat** | Peer-reviewed: 78% (Gullett 2009, n=15), ~71% (Atkins, trained men); coaches: 80–90%, "sweet spot" 83–85% (btwb)\[47\]\[48\]\[49\]\[50\] | Low: quad or upper back limiting, or low-bar style; high: posterior chain or hip strength limiting\[51\] | C – depends heavily on squat style |
| **Deficit / conventional deadlift** | ~0.90–0.95 (convention) | Low: weak off the floor | D |
| **OHP / bench** | ~0.57–0.71 (self-reports on the Starting Strength forum)\[52\] | Low: press technique or shoulder ROM | D – no peer-reviewed data |
| **Deadlift / back squat** | ~1.1–1.25 (Barbell Physio: 125%)\[53\] | Close to 1: hip-dominant or weak squat; >1.3: weak squat | D |

**Own-ratio tracking vs population norms:** tracking a user's own ratio over time is more useful (D, strongly implied by the B-level evidence). Population ranges are wide, depend on style (low vs high bar, grip, arch) and limb length,\[54\] and the norms come mostly from CrossFit and non-peer-reviewed sources. A user whose paused bench falls from 0.95 to 0.90 of their touch-and-go over 8 weeks has an actionable signal. A user who sits at 0.90 permanently may simply be built that way.

## Integrating Specialization into a Full Program
- **Length:** 4–13 weeks (Smolov 13, Coan 10, Smolov Jr 3–4).\[29\]\[34\] App default: 6–8 weeks.
- **Frequency:** 3–4 sessions a week of the specialized lift; the other lifts at maintenance, 1–2 sessions a week at about a third of normal volume.
- **Fatigue:** watch readiness, RPE at a fixed load and joint pain. Stop if pain is ≥4/10 for 2 sessions or readiness falls 3+ weeks in a row.
- **Wrong answer when:** the user is a novice still progressing linearly; there's a cut or bodyweight loss; the user is injured;\[55\] a meet or test is less than 4 weeks away; or the stall is explained by fatigue (D).

| Lift | Best evidence / consensus | Popular but weak transfer |

## Constraints

### Machine or no-barbell equivalents
- **Squat:** hack squat or pendulum squat for front squat; Smith-machine pause squat; belt squat; leg press for quads.
- **Bench:** Smith bench (paused or pins); machine chest press; DB floor press.
- **Deadlift:** trap bar; Smith RDL; back extension; 45° hip hinge.
- **OHP:** machine shoulder press; Smith seated press.
- **Pull-up:** lat pulldown (heavy, 3–6 reps); assisted pull-up machine.
- **Caveat:** machine strength transfers only partly to barbell lifts (B), so tell users their barbell PRs may not track machine progress.

### Anterior shoulder instability
- **Avoid:** loaded abduction combined with external rotation (the "high-five" position), end-range horizontal abduction, behind-the-neck press and pulldown, wide-grip bench, flyes, and (per one PT clinic) incline press.\[57\]\[58\]\[59\]
- **Bench substitutes:** DB floor press (the floor limits horizontal abduction); bench with a pad or board on the chest; close-grip or reverse-grip bench; decline bench; a liftoff from a partner.\[57\]\[58\]\[60\]
- **Overhead substitutes:** landmine press; machine press with hands in front of the shoulders; neutral-grip DB press in the scapular plane; staying below 90° of abduction.\[60\]\[61\]
- **Pull substitutes:** neutral-grip pulldown or pull-up; chin-up; rows; avoid behind-the-neck pulldown.
- **App logic:** flag shoulder pain ≥3/10 on wide-grip or overhead days and suggest substitutes automatically.

## Widely Repeated Claims with Little Support
- "Most sticking points are technique, not strength" (D – coach opinion; the bench model data offers partial support).
- "Board presses and rack pulls fix lockout" (C/D – short-term parity at most; full ROM wins long-term).
- "Squats fix the deadlift" (Hales: the lifts are markedly different).\[62\]
- "Front squat should be 85% of back squat" (D – the peer-reviewed samples show 71–78%).\[47\]\[50\]\[63\]
- "Sticking points can be eliminated" (Nuckols: they don't go away).\[1\]
- "Variety is needed to keep progressing" (B – variation isn't superior, only more motivating).\[64\]\[65\]

## Caveats
- Most sticking-region studies use recreationally trained men, single sessions and EMG, not long-term training outcomes.
- The plateau cases are self-reported, have no controls, and the lifters are often enhanced or very motivated.
- The ratios are mostly coach convention; the app should collect its own user data to build norms.
- Bar speed and path can't be measured; use the questions and video prompts listed above.
