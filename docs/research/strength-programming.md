# Programs, Plateaus and Periodization: What Actually Got Lifters Unstuck (Evidence-Graded Blueprint for a Training App)

The change that comes up most often in documented long-plateau breakthroughs is **more specific practice of the stuck lift**: more weekly sets and more sessions on the competition movement at moderate-to-heavy loads, usually alongside a technique fix and a coach's outside eye. Controlled research partly backs this. Frequency and volume both have positive dose-response relationships with strength (grade A–B). The research does not support the folk remedies of "shock the muscle," constant exercise rotation, or cutting volume to break a plateau. One warning shapes everything below: the plateau case base is small and skewed toward the bench press, so the anecdote side of this report is grade C–D however consistent it looks.

## TL;DR

- **What broke long plateaus, ranked by how often it appeared across the cases:** (1) more weekly practice of the stuck lift, through added sets, added sessions, or Russian-style 2–3 heavy/medium/light exposures; (2) a technique or setup fix, often from a coach; (3) gaining bodyweight or ending a diet; (4) cutting work that caused pain and didn't carry over; (5) patience over multi-year horizons. Accommodating resistance and heavy variation helped some lifters and did nothing for others.
- **Evidence:** volume and frequency dose-response for strength is grade A. Pelland, Remmert, Robinson, Hinson & Zourdos (*Sports Medicine*, December 2025) pooled 67 studies and 2,058 participants (79.1% male, mean age 25) and found volume and frequency "have unique dose-response relationships with each hypertrophy and strength gains, with the latter exhibiting more pronounced diminishing returns." Undulating periodization beat linear in trained lifters in Moesgaard 2022 (ES 0.61) and RPE loading was slightly better than percentages in Helms 2018 (grade B). Closer to failure helps hypertrophy but not strength (Robinson 2024, grade A–B). Everything about "which named program breaks plateaus" is grade C–D.
- **For the app:** model each lifter's response with weekly hard sets per lift, frequency, top-set e1RM trend and bodyweight. When e1RM stalls for 4–6 weeks (5–6 weeks for highly trained lifters, per Barbell Medicine), the default intervention should be +1 exposure per week or +20–30% sets on the stalled lift. Pair it with a technique-video prompt and a bodyweight check, and don't switch programs.

## Key Findings

1. **Specificity plus dose is the backbone.** Pelland et al. (2025, *Sports Medicine*; 67 studies, 2,058 participants) found that volume and frequency "have unique dose-response relationships with each hypertrophy and strength gains, with the latter exhibiting more pronounced diminishing returns." Frequency independently enhanced strength but added little to hypertrophy once volume was accounted for. The breakthrough anecdotes match this almost exactly. Most of them add exposures to the stuck lift rather than new exercises (A for the dose-response; C for the link to plateaus specifically).
2. **Technique is under-diagnosed.** Dave Tate claims "roughly 70% of sticking points are really technique related, 20% are physical, and the remaining 10% are mental," based on seminar experience (D).\[1\] Andy Bolton's 692 → 754 lb bench came after coach Bill Crawford widened his foot position and had him touch the chest every session (C, single case).\[2\]
3. **Proximity to failure matters for size, not strength.** Robinson et al. (2024) meta-regressed 55 hypertrophy and 67 strength studies. Hypertrophy improved as sets ended closer to failure, but strength had a negligible relationship with RIR (A–B).\[3\] In Refalo et al. (2024), trained lifters grew similarly over 8 weeks training to failure or at 1–2 RIR (B).\[3\]\[4\]
4. **Deloads aren't growth hacks.** Coleman et al. (2024, *PeerJ* 12:e16777) ran a 9-week program in 39 young trained adults (29 men, 10 women). The deload group (n=18) took a full week off at the midpoint and the continuous group (n=21) trained straight through. The authors report that the continuous group "showed greater improvements in both isometric and dynamic lower body strength compared to DELOAD," with "no appreciable differences" in muscle size, endurance or power (B, single RCT).
5. **"Novice, intermediate, advanced" timelines are fuzzy.** Latella, van den Hoek, Wolf, Androulakis-Korakakis, Fisher & Steele (*Sports Medicine* 2024;54:753–774) analysed drug-tested IPF raw data on 9,259 lifters with up to 17 years of history. They found "the greatest strength gains were in the earliest phase of PL participation (~7.5–12.5% increase in the first year, and up to an ~20% increase after 10 years)." Multi-year stalls are therefore the norm for advanced lifters, not a malfunction (C).

**What anecdotes show versus what research supports.** The anecdotes say: do the stuck lift more often, with more total sets, mostly at submaximal loads, with tighter technique. The research independently supports more volume and frequency for strength, periodized over non-periodized training, and RPE as at least equivalent to percentages. The research does *not* test plateaued advanced lifters specifically. None of the controlled studies above recruited lifters defined as plateaued. The "minimum dose" literature (Androulakis-Korakakis 2020; 2021) shows that powerlifters can *maintain or modestly gain* on about 3–6 heavy sets of 1–5 reps per lift per week at >80% 1RM and RPE 7.5–9.5 for roughly 6–12 weeks.\[34\]\[35\] That makes low-volume blocks good for maintenance and peaking, not for driving breakthroughs.

## Part 3 — Program Breakdowns

### 3.1 Starting Strength / linear progression (beginner)
- **Macro:** A single open-ended phase lasting until stalls; then reset and move to Texas Method.
- **Week:** 3 days, A/B alternating. Squat 3×5 every session; bench/press alternate 3×5; deadlift 1×5. +2.5–5 kg per session on lower body, +1–2.5 kg on upper.
- **Progression/end:** Add weight every session. After 3 failed sessions, drop 10% and rebuild (the AnandTech forum heuristic is a 5–10% soft deload, then 10–20%).\[36\]
- **Who/evidence:** True novices. Linear progression works for beginners (A for general novice gains; C for the specific template).
- **Plateau note:** A crt1530-type path (Starting Strength → Sheiko) is the commonly reported exit.\[37\]

### 3.2 Jim Wendler 5/3/1 (intermediate, wave)
- **Macro:** 4-week cycles; "Leader" (volume, e.g., BBB 5×10) × 2 cycles → 7th-week deload/TM test → "Anchor" × 1.
- **Week (main lift, % of TM, TM = 90% 1RM):** W1 65/75/85 ×5+; W2 70/80/90 ×3+; W3 75/85/95 ×5/3/1+; W4 deload 40/50/60.
- **Session:** Main lift → supplemental (BBB 5×10 at 50–60% TM, or FSL 5×5 at first-set weight) → 50–100 reps each of push/pull/single-leg-core.
- **Progression:** +2.5 kg TM upper, +5 kg TM lower per cycle. Reset TM to 90% of e1RM if the AMRAP misses the target reps.
- **Evidence:** C. Popular and durable; Building the Monolith is aimed at stalled lifters (Lift Vault) (D).\[38\]

### 3.3 Juggernaut Method 2.0 (wave/block intermediate)
- **Macro:** 16 weeks: 10s → 8s → 5s → 3s waves, each 4 weeks (accumulation, intensification, realization, deload). TM = 90% 1RM.\[39\]\[40\]
- **Per-wave realization AMRAPs (% TM):** 10s at 75%; 8s at 80%; 5s at 85%; 3s at 90%. Accumulation, e.g., 10s wave 4×10 + 1×10+ at 60%; intensification works up to 2×10 + 1×10+ at 67.5%.\[41\]\[42\]
- **Progression:** Each rep beyond the standard adds load to the next wave's TM (roughly +2.5 kg/rep upper, +5 kg/rep lower in common implementations).\[6\]\[41\]
- **Evidence:** C. Block lineage, with no direct trials.

### 3.4 RTS-style RPE / autoregulated program
- **Macro:** 3–4-week blocks (volume → intensity → peak), repeated; Emerging Strategies selects variations by the lifter's response history.
- **Week:** Each lift 2–3×/week. Top single at RPE 8, then back-off sets either at a % drop (e.g., -5–10% for 3–5 sets) or "repeat to RPE 8–9" fatigue stops.
- **Progression:** Daily load follows the RPE target. Block-to-block, compare e1RM from top sets. Change one variable per block if e1RM stalls.
- **Evidence:** B. Helms 2018 found a small, likely advantage over fixed percentages. Refalo 2024 found trained lifters estimate RIR fairly accurately.\[4\]\[33\]\[43\]

### 3.5 Sheiko (high-frequency Russian)
- **Macro:** 4-week prep blocks → 4-week competition blocks. Templates #29/#30 (prep), #32 (competition), #37 (advanced), and CMS/MS large-load variants.
- **Week:** 3–4 days (advanced up to 6). Bench in nearly every session, squat 2–3×, deadlift 1–2×. Most work at 70–85%, sets of 2–5, many sets per session (crt1530 log example: squat 250×5, 300×4, 350×3×2, 395×3×5, then a second squat 350×5×5 in the same session).\[44\]
- **Progression:** Re-run with updated maxes. The crt1530 journal describes adjusting the training 1RM after PRs ("Finally using 500 for my training 1RM on squat").\[44\]
- **End:** Test at the end of a 16-week cycle, or a meet.\[37\]
- **Plateau evidence:** Cases 2, 8 and 9 (C). It matches the volume/frequency dose-response (A–B).
- **⚠ Shoulder flag:** Very high barbell-bench frequency.

### 3.6 Westside conjugate
- **Macro:** Continuous concurrent training; ME exercises rotated every 1–3 weeks; DE in 3-week waves.
- **Week:** ME lower (work up to 1RM on a squat/deadlift variation), ME upper (1–3RM on a bench variation: floor press, board press), DE lower (box squat 8–12×2 at ~50–60% plus bands/chains), DE upper (9×3 speed bench at ~50% plus bands, 3 grips). Accessories 3–5 movements for weak points.
- **Progression:** Beat ME records on each variation; DE percentages wave 50→55→60%.
- **Evidence:** C–D for raw lifters. Helped Tate (case 3), failed for cases 1 and 11. Dave Tate's 3-phase weak-point scheme comes from here: two phases hammering the weak point, then one phase on strengths for "a nice supercompensation bounce" (D).\[1\]
- **⚠ Shoulder flag:** Floor and board presses are shoulder-friendly; ME bench maxing weekly is not.

### 3.7 Calgary Barbell 16-week (block/peaking advanced)
- **Macro:** Weeks 1–4, 5–8, 9–11, 12–15 phases, then a taper week (structure from the Lift Vault revised spreadsheet).\[45\]
- **Frequency:** 4 sessions; squat 3×, bench 4×, deadlift 3× per week, counting variations.\[46\]
- **Loading:** %1RM plus RPE. Top set at RPE target, then back-offs (Lift Vault converts e.g. 3×5 @ RPE 8 to about 86%).\[12\]
- **End:** Taper and test/meet; new maxes seed the next run.\[47\]
- **Evidence:** C. Widely run; no trials.
- **⚠ Shoulder flag:** Bench 4×/week.

### 3.8 Smolov Jr. (specialization/peaking)
- **Macro:** 3 weeks (+ test).
- **Week 1:** 6×6 at 70%, 7×5 at 75%, 8×4 at 80%, 10×3 at 85%. Weeks 2–3: same, adding +2.5–5 kg (upper) or +5–10 kg (lower) each week.
- **Who:** A single lift for a stalled intermediate. T-Nation claims "not uncommon" +20 lb in 4 weeks (D).\[48\]
- **Evidence:** C (case 7).
- **⚠ Shoulder flag** if used for bench.

### 3.9 Candito 6-Week / short block programs
- **Macro:** Muscular conditioning (2 wk) → hypertrophy/strength (1 wk) → linear max OT (2 wk) → peak/test (1 wk).
- **Evidence:** C. A compact block template.

### 3.10 Texas Method (late novice/intermediate)
- **Week:** Monday volume 5×5 at ~90% of the 5RM; Wednesday light ~80% of Monday's load; Friday intensity, new 5RM/3RM/1RM.
- **Progression:** Weekly PR on Friday. A Starting Strength forum poster: "Once I started Texas Method... my bench took off again" (D).\[18\]

### 3.11 Ben Pollack hybrid / powerbuilding
- **Structure:** Conjugate-style split of strength training at "moderate levels of effort" and hypertrophy at "constantly high levels of effort." His hybrid program runs 3 blocks: high frequency, then volume and load up, then frequency and volume taper toward near-max loads.\[49\]\[50\]
- **Evidence:** D. Coherent with Robinson 2024 (effort matters for size, much less for strength).

## Part 4 — Hypertrophy Programming

**Evidence on proximity to failure (2023–2025):**
- **Robinson et al. 2024** (*Sports Med* 54:2209–2231): hypertrophy "tends to increase as sets are terminated closer to failure," while strength is not meaningfully influenced by RIR (A–B, due to estimated RIR).\[51\]\[52\]
- **Refalo, Helms, Trexler, Hamilton & Fyfe 2023 meta-analysis** (*Sports Med* 53:649–665): a "trivial advantage... to set failure versus non-failure for muscle hypertrophy" (ES 0.19, 95% CI 0.00–0.37, p=0.045). When failure was defined strictly as momentary muscular failure, the effect shrank to ES 0.12 (95% CI −0.13 to 0.37, p=0.343) (B).
- **Refalo et al. 2024 RCT** (*J Sports Sci*; trained lifters, within-subject, 8 weeks): similar quadriceps hypertrophy at momentary failure versus 1–2 RIR (B).\[3\]\[53\]\[54\] Their 2025 follow-up found that sessions to failure feel harder, with no habituation over time.\[53\] That is a real adherence cost.
- **Single-set study (Roberts et al., preprint):** training to failure in single-set routines "may modestly enhance some measures of muscle hypertrophy and power, but not strength" (C).\[55\] This fits a pattern where failure matters more as volume drops.\[56\]
- **Volume:** Pelland 2025 found hypertrophy rises with weekly sets, with diminishing returns.\[57\] Remmert et al. (2025 preprint) found per-session hypertrophy returns diminish particularly above about 11 fractional sets per muscle (B–C).\[58\]

**Bottom line: the two schools converge.** High-volume RPE 7–9 and low-volume-to-failure both work. Low volume needs failure more, and high volume can't sustain failure without the fatigue cost. For the app, default to 10–20 weekly sets per muscle at 0–3 RIR. Push compounds to 1–2 RIR and isolation work to 0–1 RIR.

**Where AMRAPs, myo-reps and failure sets fit:** Use AMRAPs as calibration (last set of an exercise in week 1 and the final accumulation week) to recalibrate RIR estimates. Use myo-reps (activation set to ~0–1 RIR, then 3–5 mini-sets of 3–5 reps on 5-breath rests) for isolation and machine work, to save time when the session set budget is high. Put true failure sets on stable, low-skill exercises late in the mesocycle (D for the specific placements; B for the underlying failure evidence).

## Part 5 — Combining Strength and Hypertrophy Across a Year

**Concurrent versus alternating.** The research shows that strength depends heavily on specific, heavy, frequent practice, while hypertrophy depends on hard sets and volume with little dependence on load or frequency. That means the two can run concurrently at the cost of total recovery budget. Most coaches still alternate emphasis rather than goals: JTS's 10s → 3s waves, Pollack's blocks, RP's hypertrophy mesocycles → strength block. There's no RCT comparing yearly concurrent and alternating structures in advanced lifters (C–D).

**Residual training effects.** Issurin's block-periodization model gives residual durations of roughly 30 ± 5 days for maximal strength, 30 ± 5 days for aerobic endurance, 18 ± 4 days for anaerobic glycolytic endurance, 15 ± 5 days for strength endurance and 5 ± 3 days for maximal speed. These figures are theoretical and widely repeated but not experimentally derived for lifting (D). Practically: don't go more than about 3–4 weeks without some heavy (>85%) exposure if you want to keep 1RM expression. Muscle mass, by contrast, is retained for weeks to months on low volume (minimum-dose literature, B).

**Retest frequency.** Test every 8–16 weeks in strength-priority profiles, using a meet or a mock meet. Otherwise use AMRAP/top-set e1RM every block. A 1RM test isn't needed more often than about 12–16 weeks in hypertrophy-priority profiles.

**Real annual layouts (as structured by the programs above):**
- **Powerlifter (two meets):** Wk 1–6 hypertrophy (RP-style) → Wk 7–22 Calgary Barbell 16 → meet → Wk 23–24 off/low → Wk 25–30 hypertrophy → Wk 31–46 CBB16 or RTS blocks → meet → transition.
- **Juggernaut year:** Three 16-week cycles plus transition weeks; each cycle's 3s-wave AMRAP serves as the retest.
- **5/3/1 year (Forever model):** 2 Leader cycles + deload/TM test + 1 Anchor, repeated about 4 times; TM tests every ~13 weeks.

## Part 6 — Template Library: Five Goal Profiles

**Where profiles genuinely need different *structures*, not just different volume ratios:**
1. **Pure strength** needs specificity on competition lifts (high frequency on the stuck lift) and a taper/peak with an actual 1RM test. Those are structural requirements, not ratios.
2. **Pure hypertrophy** needs no 1RM testing, no peaking, and exercise selection driven by stimulus per fatigue (machines, isolation work), with failure used strategically.
3. **Profiles 2–4** differ mainly in ratio, *except* that any profile wanting PRs needs a ~2–4-week intensification/peak block before testing. Hypertrophy blocks alone don't express strength (Nuckols's peak-vs-strength point; Robinson's strength–RIR finding).

| Profile | 6-month structure | 12-month structure |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Pure strength** | Wk 1–4 volume (RPE 6–8, 4–6 sets × 4–6 on the main lift, 3×/wk) → Wk 5–8 strength (3–5 × 2–4 at 80–88%) → Wk 9–11 intensification (singles RPE 8–9) → Wk 12 taper/test → repeat with variation swaps based on response | Two 12–16-week CBB/RTS-style cycles each ending in a meet/test, separated by a 4–6-week hypertrophy/GPP block; Sheiko-style frequency for the weakest lift |
| **2. Strength first, some muscle** | 5/3/1 Leader (BBB/FSL) ×2 + Anchor + deload ×2 rounds, with 6–10 weekly isolation sets per lagging muscle | Juggernaut 2.0 ×3 cycles; insert one 6-week hypertrophy meso after cycle 2 |
| **3. Even mix (powerbuilding)** | Alternating 6-week blocks: hypertrophy meso (Part 4) → strength block (top set RPE 8 + back-offs, 3×/wk per lift) → hypertrophy → strength + test at week 24 | 4 macro-blocks of ~12 weeks: each = 6 wk hypertrophy + 5 wk strength + 1 wk test; Pollack-style concurrent split as an alternative |
| **4. Hypertrophy first, occasional PRs** | Wk 1–5 meso + deload → Wk 7–11 meso + deload → Wk 13–17 meso → Wk 18–21 strength mini-block (main lifts 3–5 × 3–5 at 80–87%, accessories cut 40%) → Wk 22 test → 2 wk easy | Same 22-week pattern ×2 plus a transition; maintain main lifts at minimum dose (3–6 heavy sets/lift/wk at RPE 7.5–9) during hypertrophy mesos |
| **5. Pure hypertrophy** | 4 × 5–6-week mesocycles (MEV → MRV ramp, RIR 3 → 0) with deloads only when fatigue flags | 8–9 mesocycles; rotate exercise emphasis every 2 mesos; optional diet phase with volume held and sets trimmed ~30% |

**App logic hook:** For profiles 1–3, a stall on the main lift triggers the rank-1 intervention (+1 session/week or +20–30% sets on that lift, delivered as a Sheiko-style heavy/medium/light distribution). For profiles 4–5, a stall in rep PRs on an exercise triggers set additions up to MRV, then exercise substitution. Swap the lift itself only after two failed set-addition attempts.

## Part 7 — Constraint Check: Shoulder Instability

**Programs dependent on high-frequency barbell bench/overhead or end-range shoulder positions:**
- **High risk:** Sheiko (bench nearly every session), Calgary Barbell 16 (bench 4×/wk), Smolov Jr for bench, high-frequency bench specialization programs (e.g., a commercial "bench press six days a week" program), and T-Nation's "Bench More to Bench More" (4×/wk).\[46\]\[48\]\[59\]
- **Moderate:** Westside ME upper (weekly near-max), 5/3/1 and Juggernaut with a press day (overhead to lockout), and powerlifting arched benches with wide grips. Low-bar squat also loads shoulder external rotation/abduction.
- **Notable case:** Case 11 (three shoulder separations) failed to progress through every system. Injury status should gate plateau interventions in the app.

**Substitutions coaches use for anterior shoulder instability (grade D, clinical consensus):**
- Barbell bench → **neutral-grip dumbbell press, floor press, or 2–3-board press** (limits horizontal abduction/extension at the bottom). Use a closer grip (forefinger at or inside the rings) and tuck elbows ~45°.
- Overhead press → **landmine press** or **high-incline neutral-grip DB press**. Avoid behind-neck pressing.
- Dips → remove; replace with close-grip floor press or cable pressdowns.
- Low-bar squat → **safety squat bar** or high-bar with a wider grip.
- Frequency approach → keep the frequency benefit with **lower-ROM variations** on 2 of the 3–4 weekly bench exposures, rather than cutting frequency (consistent with Pollack's view that bench responds to frequency, plus the rank-1 synthesis).
- Pair with rotator-cuff/scapular work (external rotations, face pulls) and an end-range tolerance progression from a clinician.

## Widely Repeated but Poorly Supported Claims

1. **"Muscle confusion" / constantly changing exercises breaks plateaus.** Advice like "shock your muscles" is common, but the documented breakthroughs mostly *added practice of the same lift*.\[60\] RNT warns that program hopping "derails more people's progress than just about anything" (D against the claim).\[16\]
2. **"Deload to break a plateau."** In Coleman et al. 2024 (*PeerJ*; a full week off at the midpoint of 9 weeks), continuous training "showed greater improvements in both isometric and dynamic lower body strength compared to DELOAD," with "no appreciable differences" in muscle size (B). Nuckols notes that volume cuts produce a temporary peak rather than new strength.\[9\]
3. **"Training to failure is required for growth" / "never train to failure."** Both are overstated. Robinson 2024 shows a graded benefit of proximity for hypertrophy, and Refalo 2024 shows 1–2 RIR is about equivalent (A–B).\[3\]\[4\]
4. **"Bench more than twice a week causes overtraining."** Contradicted by Pelland 2025's frequency dose-response and by Grgic et al. 2018, where higher frequency helped strength mainly by allowing more volume (no significant effect in volume-equated studies, p=0.421). The bench-frequency breakthroughs point the same way, though shoulder history modulates it.
5. **"Accommodating resistance is the key for raw lifters."** Mixed anecdotal record (it failed for Bolton and case 11) and no strong raw-lifter RCT support (C–D).\[2\]
6. **Precise residual-effect durations (e.g., "strength lasts 30 ± 5 days").** These are theoretical model values, not measured in lifters (D).
7. **"Program X adds 20–40 lb in 6 weeks."** These are sales claims (e.g., gumroad/T-Nation copy).\[48\]\[59\]\[61\] Real case gains were about +20–60 lb over 6 weeks to 6 months, with the largest at elite level under a coach.

## Caveats

- **Thin plateau dataset.** About a dozen accounts, heavily bench-focused, many from older forums, with Reddit essentially unsampled in depth. Selection bias: people post successes. Treat the ranking as a hypothesis generator for your app's A/B logic, not as settled.
- **No study recruits truly plateaued advanced lifters.** All controlled evidence is from trained-but-not-stuck participants over 8–12 weeks.
- **Equipped versus raw.** Bolton, Tate and Westside cases are equipped-era and may not transfer to raw lifters.
- **Program specifics** (5/3/1, Smolov Jr, Sheiko, Texas Method, Candito percentages) come from the authors' published templates and the common spreadsheets. Verify against the source books before encoding them, since versions differ (e.g., Juggernaut's per-rep TM increments vary across implementations).
- **The 2026 Bryk account** was available only through excerpt, so what he ran while stuck isn't documented.
